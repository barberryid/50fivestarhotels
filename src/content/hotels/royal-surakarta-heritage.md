## Why it is interesting

Solo (officially Surakarta) is the more reserved, less visited sibling of Yogyakarta — and for many travellers who make the effort to visit, the more rewarding one. The city has two royal palaces, the strongest batik tradition in Java, and a food culture that is genuinely its own. The Royal Surakarta Heritage sits within this world with the Handwritten Collection's commitment to properties that have a real local story.

## Why it is good value

Heritage hotels with a royal history connection, located in a culturally rich Javanese city, and available at rates that often sit below €100 — this is a combination that simply does not exist in most travel markets. The price reflects Solo's position as a secondary destination rather than any weakness in the hotel.

## Price notes

Solo is less price-volatile than Yogyakarta because it receives fewer international tourists. Rates are generally more predictable year-round, though Indonesian holiday periods and school breaks will still cause spikes.

## Location and nearby sights

The hotel is well placed for the Keraton Surakarta — the traditional royal palace — and the elegant Mangkunegaran Palace. The central market area and the city's batik workshops are key nearby attractions. Solo is roughly 60 kilometres from Yogyakarta, making a multi-city Java itinerary very practical.

## Food and things to do nearby

Nasi liwet — Solo's version of rice cooked in coconut milk with chicken and egg — is the must-eat. The city's sate stalls, traditional markets and neighbourhood warungs make eating here an affordable pleasure. Solo is also the best city in Java for traditional Javanese cultural performances.

## Best time to book

Year-round availability is generally good. The same principles apply as elsewhere in Java: avoid major Indonesian holidays for the most competitive rates.

## Watch out for

Solo is genuinely off the standard tourist trail for most international visitors, which is part of its appeal but also means fewer English-language resources, less tourist infrastructure and occasionally longer logistics for connecting onwards. Travellers who want a smooth, highly curated experience may find Yogyakarta more convenient.

## Verdict

Strong value. A heritage hotel in a genuinely compelling Javanese city, at a price point that makes it accessible to a wide range of travellers. Best suited to those who appreciate cultural depth over convenience.
